# Koor

Yes, it's Rook spelled backwards.

## Overview
Each player bids on how many points they believe they can take. The Leader is whoever wins the bid.
The Leader chooses a trump suit and a partner card. Throughout each hand, tricks are played; a trick is
one card from each player's hand.

## Setup
There are 4 players. The deck has 45 cards: 11 in each of four suits - purple, pink, orange, and brown; numbered
5 through 14 (ranked in order) and 1, which is the high card. There is also a Koor card, without a suit. Koor always assumes the
trump suit that the Leader chooses in each hand, and is between 10 and 11, meaning that 11 or higher of the trump
color can take Koor.

Deal 10 cards to each player and put 5 in the kitty in the center of the table, face down.

There are a total of 180 points in each hand. Each 5 card is worth 5 points, each 10 and 14 card is worth 10 points,
each 1 card is worth 15 points, and the Koor card is worth 20 points. All other cards are worth no points.

## Gameplay
A random player starts the bidding. The bidding goes around the table clockwise, with each player
either increasing the bid by at least 5 points, always in multiples of 5, or passing. The bidding ends when 
all players have passed or when the highest bid (180) is made. The Leader is the player who made the highest bid.

After winning the bid, the Leader picks the kitty up from the middle of the table and integrates it into his hand.
He then discards any 5 cards he wishes, to get back down to 10 cards.

Then, the Leader chooses a trump suit and a card for his partner. The partner is whoever is holding that card. He may
choose a card he himself has, in which case he is playing alone.

The Leader announces the trump suit, the card for his partner, and whether or not there are any points in the discarded kitty
(not the number of points, just whether there are any).
He does not announce if he is playing alone; that is a surprise for the players later! In any case, no one knows the identity
of the partner until it is revealed by the card being played.

The Leader starts the first trick.

When a player starts a trick, they may play any card in their hand. The suit of that card is the lead suit. 
Each player, in clockwise order, then plays a card. 
Each player, if he has a card in the suit that was led, must play a card in that suit. Note that 
the Koor card is always part of the trump suit. If a player does not have a card in the lead suit, he may play
any card he has.

The winner of the trick is the player who plays the highest card in the lead suit UNLESS a card in the trump suit
was played, in which case the player who played the highest trump card wins the trick. The winner of the trick
takes the cards in the trick and places them on the table in front of him, and then starts playing the next trick.

The winner of the last trick gets the points in the kitty, if any.

When all tricks have been played (the last card in everyone's hand), points are tallied for each player based on the
cards in the tricks that he took. The Leader and his partner (if any) have
their points combined. If those points total what the Leader bid or greater, then the Leader and his partner score
the points that they captured, together. The points of the opposition players are then totaled, and all the opposition
players get the total number of points that they captured.

Start a new round if desired, keep a total of everyone's points.
