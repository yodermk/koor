# Player Strategy

## When bidding
Remember that there are 180 points total. A bid of below about 80 is probably safe for anyone; it would take
an exceptionally bad hand and kitty for a Leader and partner to not be able to get 80 points.

A good hand is many cards of one suit, especially if it contains many of the high cards (Koor, 11, 12, 13, 14, 1).
High cards (1, or 1 and 14) of an additional suit also add to the expected value.

Lower cards, even point cards like 5, are not worth much unless they are in your trump suit. You might well
lose them.

Example: For five cards in one suit, if most of them are high cards, it may be safe to bid 140 or more.
If few are high cards, 100 might be safer.

## After winning the bid and becoming Leader
After picking up the kitty, you discard 5 cards into the kitty, and when doing that, try to get rid of a suit 
or two that you don't have many cards in, so that you may be able to trump tricks that are led in that 
suit. But never discard high cards, especially a 1, or a 14 and 1, of any suit.
But if you have a 14 and NOT a 1, you may wish to discard the 14, otherwise the 1 could take it, UNLESS you call
the 1 card of that suit as your partner.

Obviously, the trump suit is the one you have the most cards in, and if there is a tie, the one where your cards are
highest. Remember that the Koor card is always in the trump suit.

When choosing a partner card: If you don't have the 1 of the trump suit, you almost certainly want to call that.
Otherwise, you might choose the highest trump card you do NOT have. If you have several high trump cards, and you have
a 14 or another point card of a different suit, you may wish to call the 1 card of that other suit to protect it.
If you have 6 or more cards in the trump suit, including the 1, and especially if your bid wasn't too high, you may
wish to call one of your own cards as your partner. Call a high card, so that no one immediately suspects that
you're calling yourself.

## When starting a trick
At first, if you're the Leader, you probably want to play your highest trump card, assuming it's a 1 or your partner
is the 1. Then go down from there in trump until other players are out of trump. You don't want them to trump future
tricks. The idea is to be the only one left who has trump cards. Then lead high cards of other suits.
But do note what has been played. I you don't know where the 1 of another suit is, you probably don't want
to put out cards that are worth points in that suit. At some point, you will probably have to throw down a random
card you have left and hope for the best. Don't forget to save something to take the last trick if you put points
in the kitty.

If you're not the Leader, try to get a sense of what has been played. Put down points if you have them and think
the Leader might not be able to either trump it or put a higher card in that suit.

When playing a card from your hand into a trick that someone else has started, also get a sense of what is there.
Are you (or your partner, if known) able to win the trick, from what you know at that point? If so, try to put points down.
Otherwise, try to put down a card that is worth no points.
